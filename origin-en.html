The legacy of Venerable Elder Zongcai

In 2003, Venerable Elder Zongcai of Pure Land Temple in Los Angeles founded the organization that is today Beacon Star Foundation. He practiced with rare diligence all his life, living alone in a mountain hut for two or three decades without once coming down. He lived simply and wasted nothing. He was warm, easy to talk to, and never acted like anyone important.

In 2022 the Elder passed away peacefully, at the age of 103.

After his passing, the organization faced dissolution. Its administrator, the lay devotee Yanjing, asked Venerable Xingren Shi to take it on.

“Then let me be the one who carries the flame onward, and let all of us keep this lamp burning together.” — Venerable Xingren Shi
In His Words 法師的話

Venerable Xingren Shi

Abbot & President, American Buddhist Fellowship
Founder, Beacon Star Foundation

Aug 2026 · Excerpts from a Dharma talk

The beacon still stands, but the one who keeps it must pass the flame on. Let me be the one who carries it forward. — Venerable Xingren Shi

The Dharma is like a lighthouse on the open sea: it shows the lost their way, it gives light, it gives hope. And the small kindness and aspiration each of us carries is like a star in the night sky — seemingly insignificant, yet always shining. That was the Elder’s original intention in founding this work.

After the Elder passed away, the organization was to be dissolved. Yanjing, who was administering it, asked whether I would take it on, and I gladly agreed. The Elder had spent his whole life lighting a single beacon. I kept the English name unchanged, to carry on what he left us; the Chinese name I changed to Xingxing Zhi Huo (星星之火), “a single spark,” to open a new hope for what comes next.

The name comes from a teaching on the four small things that must not be underestimated. In the Āgama sūtras the Buddha cautions us that four things in this world should never be taken lightly:

  1. 01 A young dragon　small now, but grown it can overturn rivers and seas.
  2. 02 A young prince　a child still, but of royal blood, and one day he will take the throne.
  3. 03 A young novice　junior in years, but with diligent practice he will in time realize the path.
  4. 04 A Spark　the smallest flame is not to be dismissed; a spark can set a whole plain alight.

We chose the name as a reminder: never hold back because your own strength seems small, never pass over a good deed because it seems minor. A dollar given today, an hour of help, a single recitation of the Buddha’s name, even a dedication of merit that the home may be completed sooner — each of these is a spark. When thousands upon thousands of us do the same, that small fire is enough to burn away the world’s indifference, its selfishness, its unknowing dark.

The Elder’s wish was this: light reflecting light, lamp passing to lamp, one beam taking up where another leaves off. Then the setting sun is no longer an ending, but the dawn of another kind of meaning. May each of us, under the light of the Dharma’s beacon, become one who keeps the lamp — a star that shines, a lamp that burns itself to light the way for others.

I seek no comfort for myself; may all beings be freed from suffering.

The mission now is a home where monks, nuns, and lifelong lay devotees can grow old peacefully, in a place of practice.
