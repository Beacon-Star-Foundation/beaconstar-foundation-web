Where this began

Over many years of teaching I saw a monk pass away in an ordinary nursing home with no one to care for him. I saw many elders end their lives alone and afraid, without vegetarian meals, without Dharma teaching. I remember a fellow monk whose passing left his elderly mother with no one to depend on; only a temple-run home took her in.

This taught me something I cannot unlearn: a Buddhist home for elders is not only shelter. It is a place where compassion is practiced and the bodhisattva path is carried through. When monastics are present to chant and to teach at the bedside, the dying are steadied and calmed, and their passing is helped. That is why Beacon Star Senior Living exists.

To watch over someone is not just to hold a torch high toward the distance. It is to bend down, so that a small light falls exactly on the face in front of you that needs warmth. — Venerable Xingren Shi

Care, recitation, chanting at the bedside, companionship, and passing — held together in one place

Five Commitments

  1. 01 Daily living and nursing care Assistance with daily living, meals, medical and accessible care. Fully vegetarian.
  2. 02 A hall for recitation practice The Buddha’s name is chanted daily, sustaining a quiet environment for practice.
  3. 03 Hospice chanting support Trained chanting support and end-of-life care, so that passing may be peaceful.
  4. 04 A harbor of compassionate care Dharma teaching, emotional support, and care through illness and pain.
  5. 05 For Buddhists far from home An answer to growing old with no one to turn to, and to dying without help — so that a life of practice has somewhere to come home to.

Dedicated funds for designated use

The Foundation is already operating. Make checks payable to Beacon Star Foundation and mail them to the temple address below. Every gift is a restricted gift: it goes in full to building and running Beacon Star Senior Living, and to nothing else.

Construction and interiors
Structural work, interior build-out, fire protection and life-safety systems.
Medical and accessibility
Nursing station equipment, call systems, elevators, ramps and handrails.
Permitting and professional advice
Applications through health, fire and environmental agencies, plus legal and policy counsel.
Long-term operations
Steady funding for medical and nursing staff, kitchen, and care volunteers.
